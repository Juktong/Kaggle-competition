# Mismatch audit — `54878409` public 7.953 vs `54844628` 7.891 (2026-07-21)

The anisotropic candidate scored **7.953**, i.e. **0.062 worse** than the banked slot, while OOF over 760
wells predicted **+0.1802 better** and the visible-well check predicted **+0.181 better**. This audit
records the diagnosis. Script: `scripts/mismatch_audit.py`.

## 1. The primary error was mine: the bootstrap measured the wrong quantity

The gate required "a stably positive bootstrap", and I reported **5th percentile +0.0490, 99% positive**.
That statistic resamples **760 wells** — it is the sampling distribution of a *760-well mean*. The
competition evaluates **3 wells / 14,151 rows**. Recomputing the same gain at the evaluation's actual
scale:

```
resample 760 wells: mean +0.1794  5th +0.0479  25th +0.1234  frac<0   0.9%   <- what I reported
resample 100 wells: mean +0.1729  5th -0.1441  25th +0.0280  frac<0  20.4%
resample  20 wells: mean +0.1701  5th -0.5020  25th -0.0784  frac<0  32.5%
resample  10 wells: mean +0.1608  5th -0.7752  25th -0.1236  frac<0  35.9%
resample   5 wells: mean +0.1294  5th -1.1041  25th -0.2112  frac<0  40.7%
resample   3 wells: mean +0.1053  5th -1.3090  25th -0.2443  frac<0  42.0%   <- the real scale
```

At the evaluation's actual size, **42% of draws are negative** for the same underlying effect. The
stability condition in the pre-registered gate therefore never constrained the quantity that decides the
outcome. Every "bootstrap 5th > 0" judgement made earlier in this queue — including the one that held
back the top-K candidate — was computed at the wrong scale.

## 2. The per-well effect is small relative to per-well variance

```
per-well gain: mean +0.099   median +0.000   std 1.301
wells hurt 38.7%   helped 47.8%
percentiles: 5th -1.66 | 25th -0.32 | 75th +0.51 | 95th +1.94
```

The **median well gains exactly 0.000**. The pooled +0.18 is produced by an asymmetric tail, not by a
broad shift. An effect with this shape cannot be resolved by three wells.

## 3. A hypothesis I had, and had to discard

I expected the three test wells to fall in the known weak subgroup (wells whose closest candidate mate is
a dropped near-twin, pooled gain −0.4587 on train). **They do not:**

```
000d7d20 closest_all=291.7  00bbac68 closest_all=343.6  00e12e8b closest_all=354.5   (all > 150 ft)
```

The `closest_all = 0 ft` seen in the smoke is a different situation: at *inference* each test well has its
own train copy present as a mate at ~0 ft, which `MIN_SEP` drops. In *OOF* the same well excludes itself
by name. After the surviving-mate gate fix these two situations are equivalent, which is why the gate fix
was correct — but it means the twin subgroup does not explain this result.

## 4. OOF for these very wells pointed the right way and still missed

```
000d7d20: dep 3.622 -> cand 3.334  gain +0.287
00bbac68: dep 5.066 -> cand 4.526  gain +0.539
00e12e8b: dep 5.181 -> cand 5.201  gain -0.019
POOLED (3 wells, OOF)             gain +0.2906
```

So the failure is not "the candidate is bad on these wells according to local evidence". Local evidence
said +0.29 on exactly these wells. The gap comes from the train copies having a **different heel/toe
split** than the test versions (test `TVT_input` is known on 26.4% of rows), so the OOF per-well gain is
not the test per-well gain — the visible-well check was never the independent confirmation it was treated
as.

## 5. What this implies for the final-2 decision

The whole test set is 14,151 rows across **3 wells**, so public and private are **row splits of the same
three wells** — not different well draws. Residual autocorrelation along a wellbore is +0.9998 at lag 1
and +0.9497 at lag 100 (`reports/mtp_sequence_probe_2026-07-21.md`), so public and private on the same
wells are strongly correlated.

That makes the public result **materially informative about private**, rather than dismissable as
small-sample noise. On the evidence available:

- **`54844628` (7.891) remains the honest slot.** It is better on the only signal computed from the
  actual test wells with the actual test heel/toe split.
- `54878409` (7.953) has the better 760-well OOF but is worse where it is measured, on the same wells
  that will decide the private ranking.

## 6. Corrections to carry forward

1. **Bootstrap at the evaluation's scale, not the training set's.** A gate condition stated on a 760-well
   resample is not a statement about a 3-well evaluation. All future stability claims must resample the
   number of wells actually scored.
2. **The visible-well check is not independent confirmation.** It reuses train copies with a different
   heel/toe split. It is a sanity check for format and gross error, not evidence of transfer.
3. **A large OOF gain over many wells does not imply a gain on a 3-well test.** With per-well gain
   median 0.000 and std 1.301, expected-value reasoning over 760 wells is close to irrelevant to a
   3-well realisation.
4. The top-K line was held back for failing a stability test computed at the wrong scale. That decision
   should be revisited on its merits rather than treated as settled — though note it also fails on the
   corrected scale, and its per-well profile (45.7% hurt) is worse than this candidate's.
